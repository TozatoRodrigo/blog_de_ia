Today’s story is less about a new model and more about what keeps an AI product going after it gains traction. Character.ai has filed for Chapter 11 protection to sell the company under court supervision. I looked at that story alongside seven items on the radar, from Gemini 4 Argon to Anthropic’s redesign of version control.

Anyone who works in product learns early that engaged users alone do not pay the bills.

## What Chapter 11 reveals about an AI product

Character.ai is one of the world’s best-known consumer AI platforms. The app remains online, subscriptions are still active, and the sale is expected to conclude by late November or early December. If no buyer emerges, the platform will shut down. Users can already export their data. In [the company’s announcement](https://blog.character.ai/an-update-on-character-ai/), Character.ai points to a year of legal, regulatory, and operational pressure.

That stands out because the product had plenty of traction. Being wanted was not enough to make it sustainable.

I look at this from the product side, as someone who uses AI every day. With generative AI, every interaction has a cost, every conversation carries risk, and every design choice can become a regulatory question. Those considerations belong in the first feature design, not only after a product has scaled. [AI product management](/en/guides/ai-product-management/) also means understanding usage economics, including the [cost of AI agents](/en/guides/ai-agent-cost-management/).

## Provider dependence belongs on the roadmap

When a product depends on one platform, one model, or one provider, a fallback plan stops being a detail and becomes part of the roadmap. Data portability, provider switching, and transparency with users are product decisions.

That calls for governance from the start, with clear criteria for evaluating risks and consequences. The guides to [AI governance](/en/guides/ai-governance/) and the [AI risk matrix](/en/guides/ai-risk-matrix/) can help structure that discussion.

None of this is a reason to slow down. I believe AI will keep making room for better, smarter products. The winners will pair a good experience with a sustainable business model and mature governance.
