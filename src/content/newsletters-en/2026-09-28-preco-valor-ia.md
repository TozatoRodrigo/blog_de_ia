When AI reduces the time needed to deliver a service, billing only for the hours worked can put margins under pressure. The question becomes what the customer is actually paying for — and how the price should reflect that value.

There was a model launch almost every day this week, but the story that stayed with me was about law firms in the United States. I chose it to open today’s edition because the issue goes well beyond law.

Whenever an AI tool promises to do in minutes what used to take hours, one question quickly follows: does the price change too?

## When AI speeds up the work, should pricing change too?

A story this week brought that question into US law firms. As AI speeds up legal work, clients have started asking for discounts on their bills because work that used to support hourly charges is now delivered much faster.

This is not limited to one industry. Any service-based product will face the same dilemma when AI reduces the effort required to deliver value.

From a product perspective, this is a question I keep coming back to. When production costs fall, prices cannot stay tied only to the time spent. In areas such as receivables and structured credit, where I work on products that increasingly depend on automation to scale, this conversation already comes up regularly. Automation is not just about cutting internal costs; it also means rethinking what the customer is actually paying for.

## What changes for the roadmap and margins

It changes how we think about the roadmap. A product team that only optimizes delivery without reviewing its pricing model risks delivering more value and still losing margin: customers notice the efficiency before the team rethinks what it charges. This review belongs in [product management](/en/guides/product-management/) and [AI product management](/en/guides/ai-product-management/), where roadmap choices connect to the outcomes a product needs to deliver.

The upside is that this forces a healthy discussion: pricing around outcomes, value delivered, or risk reduced instead of simply charging by the hour or by usage. It is an invitation for product teams to consider more mature models before the market asks first.

If you want to learn more about this shift in the US legal market, here is the [full New York Times article](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/26/business/dealbook/ai-law-discount-billable-hour.html).
