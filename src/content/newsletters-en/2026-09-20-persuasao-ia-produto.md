AI chatbots are becoming more persuasive than humans in opinion conversations. For product teams, that can reduce friction and help people move through complex journeys — but it also increases responsibility for how that influence is used, especially when the decision involves money.

Today’s radar was packed: ten stories made it through the filter, from a Chinese model putting pressure on prices to a security failure that caught leading coding agents. But the story that really made me stop was a study about how persuasive AI chatbots have become, so that is where I am starting today’s edition.

## What the research shows about AI persuasion

A recent study made me stop and think about something we do not discuss enough when we talk about AI in everyday life: these models’ ability to convince someone of something.

The research shows that AI chatbots are becoming more persuasive than humans in opinion conversations. It is not about persistence or repeating an argument. It is fine-grained personalization: the model reads the other person’s tone, adjusts its language and chooses the right angle for each individual.

If you want to understand the research in more detail, [read the study on AI chatbot persuasion](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-chatbots-are-becoming-experts-changing-people-s-minds-what-s-their-secret).

## Persuasion is also a product decision

From a product perspective, this kind of data matters a lot. A well-designed conversational AI experience reduces friction, improves retention and helps users move through journeys that still depend on human support today. That is a real gain, and I find it genuinely encouraging.

But that gain comes with a responsibility that cannot be outsourced. If an AI agent can influence a decision more effectively than a person, the design criterion cannot stop at “does it work well?”. It also has to include “is it fair to use it this way with the user?”.

Consistent [AI governance](/en/guides/ai-governance/) helps turn that question into a product criterion, with decisions, owners and boundaries visible from the start.

## In finance, helping cannot become pushing

This applies to any digital product, but finance requires extra care. When a conversation involves a payment, credit or anything that affects someone’s money, the line between helping someone make a better decision and pushing them toward a decision becomes very thin.

I work on the product side, not on credit underwriting itself, but this debate is already routine for anyone designing AI-powered digital journeys. The technology is evolving quickly. The standard of responsibility for how it is used needs to keep pace.

An [AI product management approach](/en/guides/ai-product-management/) helps teams treat influence, context, accountability and measurement as product decisions. In higher-impact workflows, an [AI risk matrix](/en/guides/ai-risk-matrix/) helps organize the level of control required. And the [AI for Product Managers guide](/en/guides/artificial-intelligence-for-product-managers/) helps evaluate opportunities without separating experience, evidence and responsibility.

## In brief

- AI chatbots can already adapt language and approach to persuade more effectively in opinion conversations.
- This capability can reduce friction and improve journeys, but it does not remove the responsibility of the people designing the product.
- In payments, credit and other financial decisions, it is essential to distinguish decision support from undue influence.
- Governance and fairness criteria need to keep pace with persuasion from the moment the experience is designed.
