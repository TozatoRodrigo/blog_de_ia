Whenever an AI story talks about “checking whether something is real”, my mind goes straight to the world of receivables.

## Provenance is no longer just a technical detail

This week Anthropic launched a [free tool for checking whether an image was made with Claude](https://claude.com/check-content). You upload the file, it checks content credentials based on an open standard called C2PA and tells you whether it went through the company’s AI. It does not identify who used it, only whether it was used.

It sounds like a technical detail, but it is not. It is a signal of where the content industry is heading: the easier it becomes to generate anything with AI, the more valuable it becomes to prove the origin of a document.

## The parallel with financial documents

I live this every day on the credit-product side. Receivables, invoices, tax documents, payment slips. The challenge has never been only making a decision with the right data. It has also been making sure that the data is genuine, that the document behind the transaction actually exists and was not fabricated.

Today, that still depends heavily on process, legacy-system integrations and a measure of trust across the chain. Seeing an open provenance standard gain traction in generative content makes me optimistic that the same logic will reach financial documents with force: origin credentials, verifiable signatures and an auditable trail from issuance to assignment.

## Provenance belongs in product design

This is not about distrusting everything. It is about building products with the understanding that, increasingly, we will need to prove that something is real before trusting it.

For anyone working on [AI governance](/en/guides/ai-governance/), this means treating origin, evidence and traceability as part of the control system. The [AI risk matrix](/en/guides/ai-risk-matrix/) helps organize which documents require stronger validation, while [AI product management](/en/guides/ai-product-management/) connects that infrastructure to experience, prioritization and accountability decisions.
