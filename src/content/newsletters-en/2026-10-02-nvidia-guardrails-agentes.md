This week, the conversation around AI agents caught my attention more than the stream of new models. Here’s the lead story, followed by the full roundup I filtered for you.

When someone on a product team considers running an AI agent inside a company, the first question is rarely “Is the model good?” It’s “Who makes sure it only does what it’s supposed to do?”

## The product question is now about control

That question came to mind when I saw NVIDIA announce its Agent Toolkit, an open platform for companies to build and put autonomous agents into production. The detail that stood out to me is that it comes with a runtime environment that has guardrails and security policies built in.

That matters because the conversation about agents is moving. The question used to be whether it could be done. Now it is how to do it with control, limits on actions, and a record of what was decided. People who work in product know this is not a technical footnote; it is a launch requirement. It is closely tied to [AI governance](/en/guides/ai-governance/).

The toolkit also brings together open models, a framework for building agents, and integrations with tools many companies already use, such as Adobe, Salesforce, and ServiceNow. In practice, that can mean less time and cost between a prototype and something the security team approves.

## Where product managers make a difference

From a product perspective, I see a huge opportunity. As the infrastructure gets more ready, teams can spend more energy on what truly differentiates them: understanding the user’s problem, designing the right workflow, and deciding where an agent can act on its own and where a person needs to confirm. That is part of [AI product management](/en/guides/ai-product-management/) and calls for thoughtful [AI agent operations](/en/guides/ai-agent-operations/).

I am optimistic about this shift. Agents that automate repetitive tasks in areas such as finance, operations, and customer support will only scale if trust is built in. And it seems the market is starting to understand that.
